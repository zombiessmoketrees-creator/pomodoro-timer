## Script

> [!pixel] night-road
> Two couples. One dark road. And something with glowing red eyes that, they said, would not let go.

> [!pixel] tnt-area
> Late on November 15, 1966, Roger and Linda Scarberry and Steve and Mary Mallette drove past an abandoned explosives plant.

> [!pixel] mothman
> They said they saw a grey creature shaped like a man, seven feet tall, with wings and glowing red eyes.

> [!pixel] chase
> They said it followed their car toward town at around 100 miles an hour. They went straight to the sheriff's office.

> [!pixel] newspaper
> The next day, the Point Pleasant Register told of a man-sized bird. Newspapers soon called it Mothman.

> [!pixel] town
> Over the next 13 months, more than 100 people in the area reported seeing it. Fear settled over the river town.

> [!pixel] bridge
> Then came December 15, 1967, evening rush hour. The Silver Bridge, linking Point Pleasant to Ohio, was busy with cars.

> [!pixel] collapse
> The bridge fell into the river and 46 people died. Investigators traced it to a tiny crack in one steel link.

> [!pixel] mothman
> After the collapse the sightings largely stopped. Many locals came to see the creature as a warning.

> [!pixel] crane
> Skeptics disagree. Biologist Robert Smith said the description matched a sandhill crane, a tall bird with red patches around its eyes.

> [!pixel] town
> John Keel's 1975 book, The Mothman Prophecies, and a 2002 film kept the legend alive.

> [!pixel] statue
> Today, a 12-foot steel statue stands in town, and every September the festival returns. Whatever they saw, Point Pleasant has never forgotten.
